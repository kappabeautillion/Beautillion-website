Marcus Johnson — Professional Portrait
Alumni Spotlight

Marcus Johnson

Beautillion Class of 2015 · “Heirs to the Throne”
Academy ClassClass of 2015
CollegeHoward University
NowAssociate Attorney

Marcus Johnson walked into his first Academy session as a quiet fifteen-year-old who had never worn a tie. He left the program four years later having debated on a state stage, coordinated a canned-food drive that fed dozens of families, and decided — for the first time with real conviction — what he wanted his life to stand for.

"The Academy didn't just teach me how to tie a bowtie," he says now. "It taught me how to walk into a room I didn't feel ready for and stay anyway." That lesson carried him through Howard University and into Georgetown Law, where the same discipline the program instilled — show up prepared, listen before you speak, represent something larger than yourself — became the foundation of his legal career.

Today Marcus returns to the Academy each fall to run a mock-trial session for the newest class of Beaux. He credits the program's mentors with showing him what was possible before he could fully see it himself, and he's committed to doing the same for the next generation.

Dr. Jordan Wells — Professional Portrait
Alumni Spotlight

Dr. Jordan Wells

Beautillion Class of 2011 · “A Few Good Men”
Academy ClassClass of 2011
CollegeMorehouse College
NowFamily Physician

Growing up, Jordan Wells rarely saw a doctor who looked like him. During his Academy years, a community-service placement at a local clinic changed the direction of his life — he still remembers the moment a pediatrician there let him sit in on a well-child visit and explained, patiently, what she was doing and why.

"That afternoon planted something," Jordan says. "The Academy is what gave me the confidence to think a kid from my neighborhood could actually become that person." He carried that conviction through Morehouse College and Meharry Medical College, and after his residency, he made a deliberate choice to open his practice back in the community where he grew up.

Jordan now hosts an annual health-and-wellness session for current Beaux, walking them through basic health literacy and answering the same kinds of questions he once had no one to ask.

Malik Anderson — Professional Portrait
Alumni Spotlight

Malik Anderson

Beautillion Class of 2017 · “Young, Black & Gifted”
Academy ClassClass of 2017
CollegeGeorgia Institute of Technology
NowSenior Software Engineer

Malik Anderson was the Beau who fixed the projector before every Academy session — not because anyone asked, but because he couldn't help himself. A mentor noticed, connected him with a summer coding camp, and by the time he graduated high school he'd already built his first app.

"The Academy taught me that being the person who solves the quiet problem in the room is its own kind of leadership," he says. That mindset followed him to Georgia Tech and into a career building software used by thousands of people who will never know his name — which, he'll tell you, is exactly the point.

He's currently developing a beginner coding session he hopes to bring back to the Academy, so the next kid who can't stop fiddling with the equipment has somewhere to point that energy.

Xavier Brooks — Professional Portrait
Alumni Spotlight

Xavier Brooks

Beautillion Class of 2009 · “The Year of the Gentleman”
Academy ClassClass of 2009
CollegeGeorgetown University
NowCity Councilman

As a member of one of the Academy's founding classes, Xavier Brooks didn't have many alumni to look up to — he was becoming the example he wished he'd had. The community-service requirement that once felt like an obligation became, over time, the thing he cared about most.

"I learned that leadership is really just a decision to stay accountable to people who are counting on you," Xavier says. After Georgetown, he came home rather than staying away, eventually running for city council on a platform built around the same neighborhoods where his Academy service hours were logged years earlier.

He now sits on the program's advisory committee and makes it a point to attend as many gala ceremonies as his schedule allows, telling every new class that the Academy's real test comes long after the ceremony ends.
